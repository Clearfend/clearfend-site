The Confidence Console

Above the fold: one number, one verdict, three fixes.

The dashboard opens on the score with its provenance, a plain-English verdict, the three fixes that matter most, and what changed since your last assessment. Everything else lives below the fold. Every figure links to the screen it came from.

Confidence score, with provenance

0–100, from your connected sources and what you declare. Each input is marked self-declared, observed, derived or sample. Nothing is shown as live unless a live source wrote it.

Fix Queue

One queue across compliance, vendors, AI systems, machine identities, people, code and cloud. Ranked by severity times reach. Each item carries a runbook: the ordered fix steps, grounded in a control register, not improvised.

Estate graph

What can reach what. Crown jewels are the places data and keys live; choke points are the nodes most attack paths pass through. The queue ranks on this graph, so a fix that cuts a path moves the number.

Evidence packets

An append-only, hash-chained audit record plus the current state of your five core controls and open findings, exportable by the account owner. A control with expired evidence shows stale, never green.

Leadership summary

A read-only one-pager for the board or a customer: score and movement, top risks in plain English, resolved findings, decisions waiting on an owner. Prints to a single page.

Connectors In build

Read-only sources you create and can revoke: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Okta, AWS. Until they land, the console runs on what you declare and on the modules that probe directly, and labels it that way.

How it works

Assess. Fix. Prove.

Three verbs, one loop. Everything in ClearFend is one of them, and every fix feeds the next assessment.

01 · Know where you stand

Connect read-only, declare what we can’t see, get the number.

You create time-boxed, read-only access. The modules probe what they can and ask you to declare the rest. The console returns one score, one verdict, and a map of what reaches what.

  • Every figure labelled with its provenance
  • Nothing installed, nothing changed
  • Access revocable by you, any time
02 · Fix what matters

Work the three fixes that move the number, runbook in hand.

The Fix Queue ranks every open finding by severity times what it can reach. Each one comes with a runbook and the consequence in plain English. Risks you choose to carry go to the Risk Register with an owner and a date.

  • One queue across every module
  • Regressions surface first
  • AI explains with citations; it cannot act
03 · Prove it

Controls that drift when the estate contradicts them. Evidence that holds.

Attest a control is in place; if a live finding later contradicts it, the control shows drifted and counts as a gap until the finding closes. The evidence packet records what was true and when, in a chain that cannot be edited.

  • SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST CSF, HIPAA on one register
  • No silent green
  • A one-page leadership summary, every figure sourced
The signature move

Fix once. Prove three ways.

One fix in your estate is simultaneously a closed finding, a control that stops drifting, and a line in your evidence packet. You never write the same thing down twice, and an auditor never sees a control that is only true on paper.

Not dashboards. Living programs.

A program remembers every entity, and tells you what changed.

A dashboard shows a number. A living program keeps one row per vendor, AI system, key or person, drives it through a lifecycle, keeps an append-only history, and shows the diff, regressions first. Its gaps land in your Fix Queue, its residual risk in your Risk Register, and every entity is a node on the estate graph. The examples below are illustrative and labelled sample.

Trust & security

How ClearFend protects your data.

Each claim below is something the code enforces today, with a plain sentence on how. Anything we are still building is labelled. We do not yet hold a SOC 2 report of our own; when an auditor is engaged we will say so here. [CONFIRM: audit status]

A closed database

Your data lives in a database with no public endpoint anywhere. The ClearFend API is its only client. People reach data through an audited migration path, never from a laptop.

How: the database sits on an internal network with no published port; every human path goes through the migrator.

Tenant isolation by construction

Every table that holds your data enforces row-level security that fails closed: with no tenant context, a query returns nothing and a write is refused. A cross-tenant canary runs in our CI on every change.

How: FORCE row-level security on every tenant table; a sealed, verified tenant entry is the only door.

Read-only access you control

You create the access, it is time-boxed, and you can revoke it any time. We never ask for passwords and never change anything in your environment. No agents on laptops today.

How: connectors are read-only by design; an endpoint agent is in build and will be labelled when it ships.

An audit trail that cannot be edited

Every change made through the console is written to an append-only log where each entry is chained to the previous one. The evidence packet re-verifies the chain before it exports.

How: a hash-chained audit log with an append-only trigger; verified on every evidence export.

AI that explains and cannot act

Our AI writes explanations and runbooks with citations to the evidence. It has no tools and no path to change a number or touch a system. Prompts are scrubbed of emails, tokens and keys before they leave.

How: reader-only agents behind one gateway; every side-effect request is refused by design.

Your data leaves when we do

Isolated per tenant, never sold, never shared, never used to benchmark anyone else. Assessment scan output is deleted within 30 days of delivery, sooner on request, in writing.

In build: per-customer encryption keys for connector secrets. Today those secrets are envelope-encrypted inside the service; customer-held keys are not yet shipped, so we do not claim them.
Design principles

Colour equals meaning. Words equal truth.

The console is a command centre, not a marketing dashboard. These are the rules every screen follows, and this site follows them too.

01

Colour equals meaning, never mood

Five severity colours and no more. Red means critical, and critical must feel rare. Nothing is tinted for decoration.

02

One glow, rationed

Teal is the one accent. In the console it is reserved for the score, the live dot and the active item, so when it appears it means something.

03

Depth from lines and steps, not blur

Hairline borders and surface steps carry the structure. Hierarchy comes from weight, not from colour.

04

Every number says where it came from

Self-declared, observed, derived or sample, on every figure. A missing number cannot render as zero. Missing evidence shows stale, never green.

05

Plain English, always

Every surface must read in business-consequence language an office manager understands. Never a jargon wall; if a term needs a paragraph, it links to the glossary.

06

Numbers are mono and tabular

A 71 and an 88 take the same width, so the eye can compare them. Sora for headings, Inter for text, JetBrains Mono for numerals.

07

Honest status on everything

Anything not generally available carries one of three labels: in build, private preview, or vision. Nothing is dressed as more than it is.

      Straight answers
      
Questions a skeptic would ask

    

    

      
How new are you, really? Do you have customers?
We’re early, and we won’t pretend otherwise. The console is working software running with design partners in private preview; it is not generally available. You’ll notice there are no logos, no testimonials, and no “trusted by” wall here. When we have numbers worth showing, we’ll publish them. What you get for being early: the published launch price locked in, direct access to the people building the product, and a real say in what ships next.

      
Is this an audit, a pentest, or a certification?
No, no, and no, and we say so in the product, on this page, and on every call. The console tells you where you stand from what is actually true in your estate and gets you ready for an auditor; it doesn’t replace one. The pen-test module runs authorized, scoped tests against your own systems; the compliance register is a readiness view with drift, not a certificate.

      
Do you install anything? What access do you need?
Read-only by default. You connect Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Okta and your cloud accounts through time-boxed, read-only access you create yourself. We never ask for passwords and never change anything in your environment. No agents on laptops today; an endpoint agent is in build and will be labelled as such when it ships.

      
Where does the confidence number come from today?
From what you have connected and what you have declared, and it says which. Until the read-only connectors land, most inputs are self-declared or come from the modules that probe directly (pen test, code scan), and the score is labelled that way on the console. We do not show a “live” indicator unless a live source wrote the data. Honest and partial beats confident and wrong.

      
What is a “living program”?
A dashboard shows a number. A living program remembers every entity, each vendor, AI system, key, control, person, drives it through a lifecycle, keeps an append-only history, and shows you what changed and why, regressions first. Its gaps go to your Fix Queue, its residual risk to your Risk Register, and every entity becomes a node on your estate graph. That’s the difference between a report and a program.

      
What does “continuous drift” mean for compliance?
You attest a control is in place. Later, a live finding in your estate contradicts it, say two admin accounts appear without MFA. The control now shows as drifted and your readiness score counts it as a gap until the finding closes. No control is silently green while the estate says otherwise. It’s the only honest way to claim “audit-ready.”

      
How is this different from Vanta or Drata?
They are competent products for running the paperwork side of one compliance program. ClearFend starts from your security posture, what an attacker can reach, and treats compliance as one consequence of it. It also covers what they don’t: your vendors, your AI estate and machine identities, your people, your applications, and a ranked Fix Queue that actually hardens systems. If checkbox automation is all you’ll ever need, they’ll do; if you want the whole picture on one graph, that’s us.

      
Why the emphasis on AI and machine identities?
Because the machine identities behind your automation and AI, service accounts, API keys, OAuth grants, agent credentials, are the fastest-growing attack surface, and almost nobody has an owner for each one. ClearFend inventories every AI system and every machine identity, flags the ownerless-and-privileged, the over-scoped, and the never-rotated, and drives them through a lifecycle. We govern our own the same way.

      
What happens to our data?
Your data stays yours and is isolated per tenant at the database layer. It is never sold, never shared, and never used to benchmark anyone else. Access you grant is time-boxed and revocable by you at any time; for an assessment, raw scan output is deleted within 30 days of delivery, sooner on request, in writing. The trust section says how each of those is enforced.
