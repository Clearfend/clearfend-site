Not dashboards. Living programs.

Every capability is a program with a lifecycle, a memory, and a spine.

A dashboard shows you a number. A living program remembers every entity, drives it through a lifecycle, keeps an append-only history, and tells you what changed and why — regressions first. Its gaps land in your Fix Queue, its residual risk lands in your Risk Register, and every entity is a node on your estate graph.

Vendor Risk

Your vendor inventory, assessed and re-assessed as the landscape moves.

Inventory every vendor, assess each one, then amend the assessment when their landscape changes — a new sub-processor, a breach, a lost certification. The program shows the diff, not just the new score.

Compliance · continuous drift

A control you attested “in place” drifts the moment your estate contradicts it.

SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST CSF and HIPAA on one register. Readiness is scored net of drift: if a live finding implicates a control you attested, the control shows as drifted and the scorecard counts it as a gap until the finding closes. No silent green.

Drift detected: CC6.1 — attested in place, but two admin accounts without MFA are open in your Fix Queue. Counted as a gap until they close.
Human Risk · the person engine

Every person scored on phishability × access × behavior — from real clicks.

Not a training-completion percentage. Each person’s score is anchored to their actual clicks and reports in your own simulations, weighted by the access they hold. A repeat clicker with admin access is the target attackers want most — so they rise to the top.

Training completed: ▼ risk −20 — the intervention re-scored the person and the coaching gap left your Fix Queue.

Where you stand

One honest number, with a gate behind it.

Your confidence score comes from live telemetry. Your maturity level places it on a five-rung ladder that outsiders actually hold you to. Levels are gates, not averages: you are the level whose requirements you fully meet. Great backups don’t cancel out two admin accounts without MFA.

L1
Exposed
Basic gaps are open and no one is watching. Most companies that have never had a security function start here — not because anyone failed, but because nobody was assigned to look.
L2
Covered
The foundational controls are in place. Your breach odds are meaningfully cut and the next questionnaire stops being a guessing game.
continuous
L3
Audit-Ready
Your controls are proven continuously — not “we set it up once,” but “we can show it’s been true every day.” What an auditor and an enterprise customer actually assume.
L4
Resilient
You detect problems, respond, and recover — and you’ve tested that, not just written it down.
L5
Adaptive
Validated and continuously exercised. Most companies never need Level 5 — topping out at 3 or 4 is success, and we’ll tell you so.
→

Why continuous matters. A one-time snapshot can honestly award at most Level 2. Level 3 means proof that holds every day — and that is exactly what a console watching your estate continuously exists to do. If someone offers you “audit-ready” off a one-week review, ask them how.

Straight answers

Questions a skeptic would ask

How new are you, really? Do you have customers?
We’re early, and we won’t pretend otherwise. The console is working software running with design partners in private preview; it is not generally available. You’ll notice there are no logos, no testimonials, and no “trusted by” wall here — when we have numbers worth showing, we’ll publish them. What you get for being early: the published launch price locked in, direct access to the people building the product, and a real say in what ships next.
Is this an audit, a pentest, or a certification?
No, no, and no — and we say so in the product, on this page, and on every call. The console tells you where you stand from what is actually true in your estate and gets you ready for an auditor; it doesn’t replace one. The pen-test module runs authorized, scoped tests against your own systems; the compliance register is a readiness view with drift, not a certificate.
Do you install anything? What access do you need?
Read-only by default. You connect Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Okta and your cloud accounts through time-boxed, read-only access you create yourself — we never ask for passwords and never change anything in your environment. No agents on laptops today; an endpoint agent is in build and will be labeled as such when it ships.
What is a “living program”?
A dashboard shows a number. A living program remembers every entity — each vendor, AI system, key, control, person — drives it through a lifecycle, keeps an append-only history, and shows you what changed and why, regressions first. Its gaps go to your Fix Queue, its residual risk to your Risk Register, and every entity becomes a node on your estate graph. That’s the difference between a report and a program.
What does “continuous drift” mean for compliance?
You attest a control is in place. Later, a live finding in your estate contradicts it — say, two admin accounts appear without MFA. The control now shows as drifted and your readiness score counts it as a gap until the finding closes. No control is silently green while the estate says otherwise. It’s the only honest way to claim “audit-ready.”
How is this different from Vanta or Drata?
They are competent products for running the paperwork side of one compliance program. ClearFend starts from your security posture — what an attacker can reach — and treats compliance as one consequence of it. It also covers what they don’t: your vendors, your AI estate and machine identities, your people, your applications, and a ranked Fix Queue that actually hardens systems. If checkbox automation is all you’ll ever need, they’ll do; if you want the whole picture on one graph, that’s us.
Why the emphasis on AI and non-human identities?
Because the machine identities behind your automation and AI — service accounts, API keys, OAuth grants, agent credentials — are the fastest-growing attack surface, and almost nobody has an owner for each one. ClearFend inventories every AI system and every machine identity, flags the ownerless-and-privileged, the over-scoped, and the never-rotated, and drives them through a lifecycle. We govern our own the same way.
What happens to our data?
Your data stays yours and is isolated per tenant at the database layer. It is never sold, never shared, and never used to benchmark anyone else. Access you grant is time-boxed and revocable by you at any time; for an assessment, raw scan output is deleted within 30 days of delivery — sooner on request, in writing.
